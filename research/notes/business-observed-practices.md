# Four observed business-correspondence practices

## Scope and date

This protocol covers `USAGE-BIZ-002`, `USAGE-BIZ-003`, `USAGE-BIZ-004`, and `USAGE-BIZ-006` in public Armenian business-email evidence observed from 2026-08-14 onward. It covers only modern Eastern Armenian and reformed orthography used in the Republic of Armenia.

Search engines and site search may locate candidates. Search snippets and result counts are leads, not observations. A source is retained only after its public page, document, archive record, or immutable localization resource is opened and the relevant Armenian email element and context are manually verified.

## Shared sampling and privacy rules

- Retain at least 100 distinct relevant URLs, 20 manually verified organizational independence keys, and three corpus layers for each study.
- Eligible evidence is a public business email, a verbatim public email template, a public correspondence archive, or a product-maintained example that explicitly identifies the studied string as an email element.
- Reuse one independence key for subdomains and separately branded services controlled by one organization.
- Exclude mirrors, duplicate or parameter-only URLs, spam, machine translation, inaccessible private mail, foreign-language strings, screenshots whose text cannot be verified, and ordinary prose that merely recommends a form without presenting an email element.
- Exclude Western Armenian and traditional orthography. Do not infer a relationship, layout, or email field that the opened source does not establish.
- Keep only a verification example of at most 240 characters. Never retain personal data, private addresses, entered values, complete correspondence, full articles, or full documents.
- Frequency can support a description of current practice, not a causal claim about comprehension, politeness, trust, deliverability, or task success.

## `USAGE-BIZ-002` – greeting punctuation and line breaks

Eligible strings begin with `Հարգելի` and occur in an explicitly presented business email or email template. Record the visible punctuation after the complete greeting, the body layout, and addressee type. Ordinary letters not identified as email are excluded.

Variants use `<punctuation>/<layout>/<addressee>`:

- punctuation: `comma`, `exclamation`, `none`, or `other`;
- layout: `same-line` when body text continues on the greeting line, or `new-line` when it begins on a later visible line;
- addressee: `person`, `role`, `organization`, or `collective`.

## `USAGE-BIZ-003` – subject-line punctuation

Eligible strings are verbatim Armenian email subject lines or explicit Armenian subject templates. Article titles, form headings, notification titles, and prose suggestions not presented as a subject line are excluded.

Variants are:

- `none/phrase` – a phrase without a terminal mark;
- `none/statement` – a complete statement without a terminal mark;
- `question-mark/question` – a full question carrying the Armenian question mark in its orthographic position;
- `terminal-mark/statement` – a statement ending with a visible terminal mark;
- `other` – an eligible subject outside those categories.

## `USAGE-BIZ-004` – reply and forward prefixes

Eligible strings show a prefix as part of an actual or product-generated reply or forward subject in an Armenian email context. Prose merely mentioning a prefix, duplicate copies of one thread, and non-email meanings of `Re` are excluded.

Variants are:

- `re/reply`;
- `fwd/forward`;
- `fw/forward`;
- `localized/reply`;
- `localized/forward`;
- `other`.

Reply and forward results remain separate even when the same client supplies both.

## `USAGE-BIZ-006` – closing formulas

Eligible strings are the formula immediately preceding a sender signature in a public business email or explicit email template whose relationship context is visible. Automatic legal footers, signatures without a formula, ordinary letters not identified as email, and examples with unclassifiable relationship context are excluded.

Variants use `<formula>/<relationship>`:

- formula: `respectfully`, `best-wishes`, `thanks`, `sincerely`, `none`, or `other`;
- relationship: `formal`, `neutral`, or `established-colleague`.

## Decision and publication

Each candidate remains `usage-study-required` until its observation file and deterministic aggregate pass repository validation. A qualifying aggregate can support only an evidence-bounded draft. No normative business-writing rule is added before Arman's explicit approval of the aggregate and proposed conclusion.

## Results

### `USAGE-BIZ-002` – greeting punctuation and line breaks

The search verified 114 eligible opened URLs across all five corpus layers, but they represent only 16 of the required 20 independent domains. The observation and layer thresholds are met; the independence-key threshold has a deficit of four. Ninety-nine eligible URLs came from one public mailing-list archive, so retaining them would not repair the independence deficit.

Search snippets, an attachment-only thread without a visible greeting, greetings not beginning with `Հարգելի`, duplicate quoted replies, mirrored or parameter-only copies, ordinary letters not identified as email, private pages, transliteration, Western or traditional Armenian, and machine-translated advice were excluded. No observation file or aggregate is published, and no usage claim is proposed. The candidate is insufficient under the frozen protocol.

### `USAGE-BIZ-003` – subject-line punctuation

The deterministic aggregate contains 100 observations from 45 independence keys and four corpus layers. Ninety-nine subjects are phrases without a terminal mark; one is a complete statement without a terminal mark. No eligible question subject was found.

The sample is weighted toward vacancy, competition, application, and notification templates. Article headings, form titles, prose advice, reply or forward subjects, inaccessible pages, mirrors, and search-only matches were excluded. After Arman's approval, the evidence-bounded recommendation was published as `HY-BIZ-006`: phrase and statement subjects omit a terminal mark. Question subjects remain explicitly outside the rule because the sample contains none.

### `USAGE-BIZ-004` – reply and forward prefixes

The strict search retained 0 of 100 required observations, 0 of 20 independence keys, and 0 of three required layers. English-language subjects merely concerning Armenia, romanized Armenian, forum reply markers, `Re` meaning “regarding”, Armenian body text paired with a foreign-language subject, and non-Armenian product documentation were excluded.

No aggregate is published and no usage claim is proposed. This candidate is insufficient under the frozen protocol; the absence of retained observations is not evidence that Armenian users do or do not use `Re:`, `Fwd:`, `Fw:`, or localized alternatives.

### `USAGE-BIZ-006` – closing formulas

The deterministic aggregate contains 100 observations from 20 independence keys and all five corpus layers. Respect-based formulas account for 88 observations, best-wishes formulas for 10, and thanks formulas for 2. The corpus includes formal, neutral, and established-colleague contexts.

The sample is strongly concentrated: 68 observations come from one public professional mailing-list archive and 12 from original open-source email localizations hosted on GitHub; the community layer therefore supplies 80 observations. Mirrors, copied forks, ordinary letters not identified as email, automatic footers, private mail, machine-translated advice pages, and unclassifiable relationship contexts were excluded.

The study establishes that the three recorded formula families are attested. Because the dominant share is concentrated in one layer, it does not justify a general default closing under the project’s 70% decision rule. After Arman's explicit editorial decision, `HY-BIZ-007` publishes `Հարգանքներով, Արման` as the standard polite closing. The rule identifies its basis as an editorial decision; `USAGE-BIZ-006` supports only the attestation of the recorded formula families.
